# Level 3: Head Up Flying

[Back to lesson plan index](README.md)

## Neutral and Stable Head Up Position

**Prerequisite:** Completed IBA Back-Flying Level 2 skills.

**Desired outcome:** Fly a neutral and stable head-up position at an appropriate
wind speed without unwanted movement or rotation, then maintain control while
making small corrections.

**Reference material:** IBA Flight Tutorial #29.

**Key points:**

- Begin on the net with the feet flat, knees bent about 90 degrees, thighs
  parallel to the net, and hips bent about 90 degrees.
- Keep the spine straight, shoulders slightly back, chin up, and arms open to
  the airflow with a small bend at the elbows.
- Maintain a symmetrical shape and use small corrections rather than reaching
  for the net or abruptly standing.
- Practice the neutral position before introducing daffy, knee-flying, or
  other variations used for stability and docks.
- If stability is lost, use the briefed bail to a neutral back-flying position.

**Coaching and safety:** Start with only enough wind to support the vertical
position and increase it only after control is demonstrated. Correct body
position before requesting more wind, avoid a doorway-facing heading, remain
clear of the instructor's access path, and reinforce that the recovery is to
back flight rather than belly flight.

## Head Up Forward & Backward

**Prerequisite:** Neutral and stable head-up position.

**Desired outcome:** Move forward, stop, move backward, and stop while
maintaining a stable head-up position, heading, and altitude.

**Reference material:** IBA Flight Tutorial #30.

**Key points:**

- Begin low to the net and use small, gradual movements.
- For basic forward drive, keep the lower body neutral and lean the upper body
  slightly back into the airflow; lift the knees to reduce the drive and stop.
- For backward drive, bring the chest slightly toward the airflow and stop with
  the opposing input.
- For stronger movement, coordinate the torso with controlled heel and leg
  inputs while keeping the body balanced.
- Return to neutral after every stop and bail to neutral back flight if the
  position becomes unstable.

**Coaching and safety:** Define the travel path and stopping points before the
flight. Head-up pitch can create rapid drive, so progress from slow starts and
stops, leave generous wall clearance, and increase wind or speed only after
the student demonstrates control.

## Head Up Turns

**Prerequisite:** Neutral and stable head-up position.

**Desired outcome:** Complete controlled turns in both directions with balanced
upper- and lower-body inputs, starting and stopping accurately on a selected
heading.

**Reference material:** IBA Flight Tutorial #31.

**Key points:**

- Begin in neutral at the center of the tunnel and select the stopping heading
  before rotating.
- On the net, lean slightly back on the heels; off the net, establish stable
  lift before initiating the turn.
- Shift a lower leg or foot in the direction of rotation, or angle the arms to
  deflect the airflow.
- Combine upper- and lower-body inputs for advanced turns while keeping the
  spine upright and centered.
- Start with small turns and apply the opposite input to stop before returning
  to neutral.

**Coaching and safety:** Teach one input at a time on or near the net, then
combine inputs only after the student can stop predictably. Watch for forward
lean, excessive pitch, and drift, and keep the student's bail path and the
instructor's access route clear.

## Head Up Up & Down

**Prerequisite:** Head-up turns plus forward and backward movement.

**Desired outcome:** Move up, stop, and move down within the air column without
changing heading or moving forward or backward.

**Reference material:** IBA Flight Tutorial #32.

**Key points:**

- Begin low to or on the net in a neutral head-up position.
- To move up, increase lower-body surface area by spreading the feet and
  bringing the knees closer, then add a slight rearward torso input and wider
  arms as needed.
- Balance the legs to cancel unwanted drive and return toward neutral to stop
  the climb.
- To descend, sit more upright, bring the feet and legs closer together, and
  reduce arm surface area.
- Stop the descent with a controlled upward input before contacting the net.

**Coaching and safety:** Set clear upper and lower limits, make small fall-rate
changes, and require a complete stop between directions. Correct pitch before
adding more wind or a larger altitude range, and reinforce the neutral
back-flying bail.

## Head Up Side Slides

**Prerequisite:** Head-up up-and-down movement.

**Desired outcome:** Move sideways in both directions and stop while preserving
heading, altitude, and a stable head-up body position.

**Reference material:** IBA Flight Tutorial #33.

**Key points:**

- Start in the center or at one side with the travel path clear of the doorway.
- Lower and bend the leading arm while extending and raising the trailing arm
  to deflect airflow.
- Lower and angle the leading leg while lifting the trailing leg, balancing
  upper- and lower-body inputs to prevent a turn.
- Keep the spine straight throughout the slide.
- Begin an opposing side-slide early enough to stop before the wall, then
  return to neutral.

**Coaching and safety:** Establish the direction, reference points, and stop
limit before starting. Teach the input on or near the net, increase movement
speed gradually, and watch for excessive body pitch or a late stopping input.

## Head Up Front Flip Transitions

**Prerequisite:** Head-up side slides.

**Manual scope:** This lesson plan connects back-to-head-up and head-up-to-back
transitions, a head-up-to-head-up front flip, and in-face and out-face carving.

**Desired outcome:** Complete head-up transitions smoothly at a defined level
without unwanted drive, heading change, or over-rotation, then apply the same
control while carving with consistent proximity and awareness.

**Reference material:** IBA Flight Tutorials #34, #35, #37, #40, and #41.

**Key points:**

- For back-to-head-up, drive the heels down while rotating the head and
  shoulders back, keep the hip angle near 90 degrees, and finish in neutral
  head-up flight.
- For head-up-to-back, relax and roll the shoulders forward, allow the feet to
  rise, and stop in a defined neutral back-flying position.
- For a full front flip, lower the head and upper body between the legs without
  pulling the knees toward the chest; keep the arms open and continue the
  movement until the neutral head-up position is re-established.
- Open the body at the correct time to stop on the original heading and level.
- For carving, blend a side slide, turn, and forward drive for in-face flight or
  backward drive for out-face flight. Continuously balance the three inputs to
  control radius, speed, altitude, and wall separation.

**Coaching and safety:** Begin transitions low and at a controlled wind speed,
then increase the challenge only after smooth completion and reliable stops.
Brief eye contact, proximity, and situational awareness before adding another
flyer. The student must continue through a committed transition, keep the
doorway out of the travel path, and use the neutral back-flying bail whenever
control is lost.
