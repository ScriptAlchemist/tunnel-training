## Concept talks

### Pressure shows the shape

Discuss where balanced airflow should be felt across the back, arms, and legs. A missing or uneven pressure cue often points to asymmetry before visible movement begins.

### Neutral is an active reference

Neutral is the position used to begin, stop, and diagnose movement. It is not a frozen pose; small balanced corrections keep the flyer centered and on heading.

## Input chart

| Goal | Primary idea | Self-check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hold heading | Keep shoulders, hips, and knees balanced | Pressure feels similar on both sides |
| Move and stop | Make one clear input, then return to neutral | The body stops before the next task |
| Change level | Adjust surface area symmetrically | No forward/backward drive appears |
| Transition | Define the finishing neutral position first | Rotation ends on heading and level |

## Floor drills

1. **Pressure-map rehearsal:** In the neutral back shape, name each surface expected to contact the airflow.
2. **Symmetry check:** Use a mirror or partner to compare knee width, arm position, and torso alignment.
3. **Start-stop repetitions:** Move slowly from neutral toward one planned input and return fully to neutral for five controlled repetitions.
4. **Transition finish:** Practice arriving in the target belly or back shape without continuing past it.
