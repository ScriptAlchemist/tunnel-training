## Concept talks

### Fly the relative picture

Formation flying uses the other flyer as a moving reference. Position, level, and closing speed matter more than reaching for a grip early.

### Approach before dock

A controlled formation sequence separates the approach, stop, and dock. The flyer should be stable at the correct level before reducing the final distance.

### Break-off is part of the formation

Separation direction, timing, and exit order must be clear before entry. A formation is not complete until both flyers have created safe space and exited as briefed.

## Planning chart

| Phase | Shared question | Success cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry | Who enters first and where do they hold? | Both flyers know the starting picture |
| Approach | What path and level will be used? | Closing speed remains controlled |
| Dock | Which grip or relative position is planned? | No reaching before the stop |
| Break-off | Who moves, in which direction, and when? | Separation is visible before exit |

## Briefing workouts

1. **Point-and-name:** With a partner, point to the intended entry hold, approach path, docking position, and break-off direction.
2. **Closing-speed rehearsal:** Walk slowly toward a fixed reference, stop at a predetermined distance, then take the final small step.
3. **No-grip drill:** Rehearse arriving in the correct relative position with hands remaining neutral instead of reaching.
4. **Full verbal brief:** Each flyer explains the entire sequence independently; compare the answers before flying.
