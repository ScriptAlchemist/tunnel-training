## Concept talks

### Relaxation creates options

The neutral belly position should feel supported rather than forced. Talk through how unnecessary tension reduces range of motion and makes small corrections harder to feel.

### Every input needs a stop

Movement is not finished when the body starts traveling. The flyer must return toward neutral and use a deliberate stopping input before beginning the next movement.

### References create awareness

Choose a heading reference, an altitude range, and a stopping point before flying. These references make it easier to notice drift before it becomes a large correction.

## Comfort chart

| Area | Ready to progress | Spend more time here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutral shape | Symmetrical and relaxed | Holding tension or twisting |
| Heading | Can identify a fixed reference | Losing the reference during corrections |
| Inputs | Can make small, separate changes | Moving arms and legs without a clear purpose |
| Safety plan | Understands entry, exit, and signals | Unsure about the instructor's signals or plan |

## At-home workouts

Perform these gently on a comfortable floor. Stop if a position causes pain.

1. **Neutral-position rehearsal:** Lie face down and organize the arms and legs into a symmetrical neutral shape. Hold for 15–20 seconds while breathing normally.
2. **Small-input practice:** From neutral, slowly extend and return one pair of limbs at a time. Focus on finding neutral again instead of maximizing range.
3. **Heading visualization:** Pick a fixed point in the room and rehearse looking toward it while keeping the shoulders level.
4. **Session visualization:** Mentally rehearse the instructor signal, entry, neutral position, one planned movement, a complete stop, and the exit.
