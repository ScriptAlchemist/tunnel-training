## Concept talks

### The airflow is still the reference

Back flying changes what the flyer sees, but the same ideas remain: surface area controls fall rate, balanced inputs protect heading, and every movement returns to a defined neutral position.

### Orientation before speed

The first goal is knowing where the head, hips, knees, and feet are relative to the airflow. Increase speed or range only after the neutral shape and stopping response are repeatable.

### Shared airspace requires a plan

Formation work begins before entry. Agree on levels, approach paths, grips, break-off direction, and the instructor's role before flying together.

## Readiness chart

| Area | Ready to progress | Spend more time here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Belly skills | Controls heading, level, and stops | Still needs large or late corrections |
| Back orientation | Can describe the neutral shape | Unsure where pressure should be felt |
| Transitions | Understands the intended start and finish | Focused only on the middle of the rotation |
| Formation plan | Can explain approach and break-off | No shared plan for levels or separation |

## At-home workouts

Use a comfortable surface and a pain-free range of motion.

1. **Back-position rehearsal:** Lie on the back with hips and knees bent, knees shoulder-width apart, chin up, and arms organized symmetrically. Hold for 20 seconds while breathing normally.
2. **Opposite-side awareness:** Slowly move one arm and the opposite leg, then return both to neutral. Repeat on the other side without twisting the torso.
3. **Transition walkthrough:** Rehearse the starting neutral position, the first lower-body input, and the exact neutral position that ends the transition.
4. **Formation briefing practice:** Say the planned entry, level, approach, dock, break-off, and exit sequence aloud.
