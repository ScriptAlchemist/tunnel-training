# Level 2: Back Flying and Formations

[Back to lesson plan index](README.md)

The training app groups these skills under Level 2.

## Back Flying

### Neutral Back Fly Position

**Prerequisite:** Completed IBA Level 1 Flyer Skills.

**Desired outcome:** Maintain full control in a neutral back-flying position at
a neutral wind speed, using heading and forward/backward corrections as needed
to remain positioned in the tunnel.

**Key points:**

- Keep the spine straight and torso flat to the airflow.
- Bend the hips so the thighs are approximately 90 degrees to the torso, then
  bend the knees approximately 90 degrees.
- Keep the knees shoulder-width apart, press the arms back into the airflow,
  and keep the chin up.
- Use small, symmetrical corrections to hold heading and position.

**Coaching and safety:** It is normal to begin at a wind speed that keeps the
student on the net until they can control heading and forward/backward
movement. Increase speed and altitude only with demonstrated stability.

**Links:**

- [Back Fly Neutral Body Position](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7R0FsROU2bU)

### Back Forward & Backward

**Prerequisite:** Neutral and stable back-flying position.

**Desired outcome:** Move forward and backward with correct upper- and
lower-body inputs, stop between movements, and maintain altitude, stability,
heading, and control.

**Key points:**

- Extend and retract the arms or legs to start and stop basic movements.
- Maintain a symmetrical shape and manage heading with small upper-body
  adjustments.
- Stop and return to neutral before starting a movement in the other direction.
- Combine upper- and lower-body inputs for smoother or faster advanced
  movement, then account for the additional stopping distance.

**Coaching and safety:** Inputs made on the net have a stronger effect than the
same inputs off the net. Use clear reference points and leave enough distance
for a controlled stop.

**Links:**

- [Back Fly Forward & Backward](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ptYqyClDjUE)

### Back Fly Turns

**Prerequisite:** Neutral and stable back-flying position.

**Desired outcome:** Complete controlled turns in both directions using
balanced upper- and lower-body inputs, beginning and ending accurately on a
predetermined heading.

**Key points:**

- Begin and finish in the neutral back-flying position.
- Direct airflow opposite the intended turn using the arms or lower body.
- Keep the torso flat and use the opposite input to stop.
- For advanced turns, coordinate upper and lower body to rotate around the
  center point; larger inputs create faster rotation.

**Coaching and safety:** Provide a clear heading reference, teach controlled
stops before increasing turn rate, and account for the stronger effect of
inputs while the student is on the net.

**Links:**

- [Back Fly Left/Right Turns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vppANoWdAgY)

### Back Fly Up & Down

**Prerequisite:** Back-fly turns plus forward and backward movement.

**Desired outcome:** Move up, stop, and move down in the air column without
moving forward, backward, or changing heading.

**Key points:**

- To move up, slightly arch the torso and widen or extend the arms and legs to
  increase surface area.
- Return to neutral to stop upward movement.
- To move down, slightly de-arch the torso and reduce arm and leg extension.
- Balance all inputs to avoid unwanted drive and use the arms and lower legs to
  maintain heading.

**Coaching and safety:** Avoid large changes or curling into a tight ball.
Begin with small fall-rate adjustments and keep the student's altitude within
their demonstrated ability.

**Links:**

- [Back Fly Up & Down](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VcUDWwqzx-Q)

### Back Fly Side Slide

**Prerequisite:** Back-fly up and down movement.

**Desired outcome:** Shift sideways in both directions using balanced
whole-body inputs, then stop on heading without gaining or losing altitude.

**Key points:**

- Begin in neutral at the center or one side of the tunnel.
- Combine upper- and lower-body inputs to deflect airflow evenly to one side.
- Adjust the balance of each input to maintain heading and apply the opposite
  input to stop.
- For faster slides, add torso and hip pitch and begin the stopping input
  earlier.

**Coaching and safety:** Establish the direction, travel limit, and stop point
before starting. Increase speed only after accurate heading and altitude
control are consistent.

**Links:**

- [Back Fly Side Sliding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ALbUJOBI8WE)

### Back Fly Enter & Exit

**Prerequisite:** Back-fly up and down movement.

**Desired outcome:** Enter into a neutral back-flying position and approach
and exit the doorway safely, in control, at the correct altitude, and with
minimal instructor assistance.

**Key points:**

- Begin in the doorway with the back toward the airflow and hands on the frame.
- Ease back with the chin up and torso flat, then release and extend the arms to
  manage the descent.
- Push from the floor and use balanced inputs to reach the center.
- For the exit, point the feet toward the doorway from about three feet above
  the net, move backward slowly, place the feet on the floor, and use the upper
  body to rotate upright.

**Coaching and safety:** Maintain heading with small lower-body corrections and
coordinate the entire exercise with the on-duty instructor. The student enters
only after receiving the instructor's signal.

**Links:**

- [Back Fly Enter & Exit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wDssu29hoHs)

### Walking

**Prerequisite:** Signed off by an IBA instructor as safe to begin the IBA
Flyer Progression.

**Desired outcome:** Walk safely at a neutral wind speed and stop movement in a
controlled manner.

**Key points:**

- Maintain a straight vertical posture, chin up, and eyes forward.
- Begin with small steps and keep body weight on the heels.
- Lean the upper body opposite the desired direction while keeping the hips
  over the heels.
- Use the arms cautiously; too much body pitch can rapidly increase drive.

**Coaching and safety:** Start near belly-flying wind speeds and increase only
after the student demonstrates control. Require the student to stop with room
remaining before the wall and brief the back-fly bail procedure before using
higher wind speeds.

**Links:**

- [Belly to Back Barrel Roll](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qXftXn6KMq8)
- [Back to Belly Barrel Roll](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g5sxJjlkbn4)
- [Full Barrel Roll](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RzErf8BcMaU)

### Half Barrel Rolls

**Prerequisite:** Completed all individual belly- and back-flying skills.

**Manual scope:** The lesson covers both half and full barrel-roll transitions.

**Desired outcome:** Transition between neutral belly and back positions while
holding the original heading and altitude and avoiding unwanted drive toward
the wall.

**Key points:**

- Initiate the roll with the lower body and knees before allowing the upper
  body to follow.
- Extend the arms slightly forward to keep the upper body from dropping.
- Keep the legs bent or neutral to limit forward, head-first drive.
- Stop the rotation on heading in a defined neutral belly or back position.

**Coaching and safety:** Perform early transitions low over the net. The coach
should remain on their feet, leave the spotting path open, and watch closely
for head-low pitch and forward drive.

**Links:**

- [Belly to Back Transition](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JezRB0n3zxM)
- [Back to Belly Transition](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mv7OLpMB_Xw)

### Over the Feet Transitions

**Prerequisite:** Completed all individual belly- and back-flying skills.

**Desired outcome:** Transition over the feet from a neutral belly or back
position to the opposite neutral position without excessive wall drive,
heading change, or altitude change.

**Key points:**

- Begin around waist height above the net and increase upper-body surface area
  to create lift.
- For belly-to-back, drive the knees toward the chest as rotation begins.
- For back-to-belly, keep the knees bent and drive the feet under the body.
- Prepare the stopping input before the target orientation and return to
  neutral only after rotation has stopped.

**Coaching and safety:** Early attempts require active instructor spotting.
Watch for straight legs or a continuing torso arch that causes over-rotation,
and avoid pausing vertically because that can produce a rapid descent.
