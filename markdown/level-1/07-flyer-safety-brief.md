## Flyer Safety Brief

**Reference:** IBA STN 4/23 — Flyer Safety Brief.

**Prerequisite:** Completed all IBA Level 1 flight skills.

**Desired outcome:** Understand the additional risks associated with more
advanced flying and the flyer's personal role in reducing those risks and
maintaining their own safety.

The IBA Flyer Safety Brief is the final sign-off required to achieve full IBA
Level 1 status. It prepares flyers for the increasing risk associated with more
advanced skills and must be completed before any IBA Level 2 skills are coached
or flown. It is also required as part of flyer recurrency.

This approval does **not** replace the routine, flight-specific safety briefing
given before a session. The Flyer Safety Brief establishes the overall safety
foundation; each flight briefing must still address the activity being flown,
its specific risks, how to abort a movement, and the most appropriate recovery
or bailout position.

**Additional flight risks**

**Instructor position and support**

- During coaching, the instructor will use the recognized handling and support
  position. After a skill is approved, the flyer is considered a rated flyer,
  and the instructor may supervise from another part of the tunnel or from the
  door instead of remaining in the side-body position.
- Other flyers may temporarily block an instructor's direct access to one
  flyer, delaying the physical support they can provide.
- Before every flight, discuss where the instructor will be positioned and what
  level of support and supervision to expect. This is especially important
  when returning after time away, even if the planned skill was previously
  approved.

**Wind speed range**

- Know the range of wind speeds at which you are capable and comfortable. Do
  not intentionally fly outside that range, and know what action to take if the
  speed is not appropriate.
- Progression through the IBA levels uses different and often higher wind
  speeds. At higher speeds, an unintended belly orientation can increase the
  risk of contacting the wall, net, or another flyer. Stronger drive also
  reduces reaction time.
- Understand the appropriate bailout position for the skill being flown. Know
  how to recognize and verify the wind speed, and review the planned range with
  the instructor before flying.
- Flyers may have different comfortable speed ranges. Confirm that the planned
  speed is suitable for everyone before attempting to fly together.
- Different flight suits affect the required wind-speed setting. Use a suit
  appropriate for the activity and discuss suit changes with the instructor.

**Spatial awareness, shared flights, and tunnel boundaries**

- Back-flying, head-up, head-down, and other orientations require greater
  spatial awareness to avoid the wall, door, net, and other flyers. Know the
  actions required to prevent or recover from unplanned contact.
- Flying with others reduces the available maneuvering space. Differences in
  ability and the effect of burbles increase the risk of contact with other
  flyers and the tunnel boundaries.
- Understand how to avoid another flyer's burble, how your own burble can
  affect someone else, and how to respond without creating a second hazard.
- Everyone in a shared flight must agree on a plan and communicate it to the
  instructor. The plan must include the skills, wind speed, entrances, and
  exits.
- Taking a grip in a formation further increases the risks created by reduced
  space, proximity, and shared movement.

**Additional flyer responsibilities**

**Personal safety**

Safety is a collective responsibility. Take responsibility for your own
actions and work with the instructor to create the safest environment possible
for yourself, the other flyers, and the instructor. This responsibility becomes
more important when performing approved skills without direct physical support.

Review the IBA article **“Flyer Safety—Be Part of the Team”** as an additional
reminder of the flyer's role in maintaining safety.

**Communication**

- Maintain clear communication with the instructor during briefings and
  throughout the flight.
- Ask when anything is unclear. In the wind, shake your head to signal “no” if
  you are confused or do not understand an instruction.
- Discuss your experience, currency, comfort range, equipment, and any concerns
  before entering the airflow.

**Following instructor directions**

- Review the instructor's hand signals during the briefing.
- Wait for the signal before entering the airflow.
- Wait for the signal before beginning a maneuver or transition.
- Adjust your position only when directed.
- If a direction is unclear, signal the instructor and do not initiate the
  movement until both of you share the same understanding.

**Logging flight skills**

Use an IBA flight logbook and keep approvals current on the IBA website. The
instructor uses the logbook to confirm a flyer's approval level before flight;
maintaining an accurate record supports the overall safety system.

**Maintaining currency**

Understand the currency requirements and what happens when currency lapses. A
previous skill approval does not guarantee that the skill can be flown without
limits after time away. A non-current flyer may be restricted in what they can
fly and should expect increased instructor supervision.

**Flying within the approved level**

Once approved to fly without direct instructor or coach support, remain within
that approval level. Do not attempt an unapproved skill without appropriate
instructor support.

**Avoiding last-minute movements**

When the end-of-rotation lights flash, stop the planned activity and prepare to
exit. Do not rush or attempt an unplanned “one more” movement. Time pressure can
compromise safe execution; completing another skill is never more important
than a controlled exit.

**Fit to fly**

Assess your physical and mental readiness before flying. The IBA brief uses the
**IMSAFE** checklist:

- **I — Illness**
- **M — Medication**
- **S — Stress**
- **A — Alcohol**
- **F — Fatigue**
- **E — Emotions**

Tell the instructor about any uncertainty that could affect your ability to fly
safely.

**Flight gear**

- Select a flight suit appropriate for the planned activity and wind speed.
- Bootie-style formation-skydiving suits can create increased drive. Discuss
  their use with the instructor before flying.
- Confirm that the helmet and all other personal protective equipment fit and
  fasten correctly.
- Understand how loose- and tight-fitting suits affect the required wind speed.
- Discuss any new suit or other new flight gear with the instructor before the
  flight.

**Sign-off standard**

The instructor should deliver this brief after all Level 1 flight skills are
complete and before coaching begins on Level 2 skills. The sign-off confirms
that the flyer understands:

1. Why advanced skills introduce additional risk.
2. How instructor position, wind speed, spatial awareness, proximity, burbles,
   and shared flights affect that risk.
3. Their responsibility to communicate, follow directions, maintain accurate
   approvals and currency, fly within those approvals, assess fitness, use
   suitable equipment, and avoid rushed or unplanned movements.
