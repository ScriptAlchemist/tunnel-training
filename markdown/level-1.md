# Level 1: Belly Flying

[Back to lesson plan index](README.md)

## Neutral Belly Position

**Prerequisite:** Signed off by an IBA instructor as safe to begin the IBA
Flyer Progression.

**Desired outcome:** Fly at a neutral wind speed with full control in a stable,
neutral belly position and without excessive movement or rotation.

{{visual:tunnel-axis}}

**Key points:**

- Maintain a symmetrical arch with the head raised and legs shoulder-width
  apart.
- In the basic position, keep the arms near 90 degrees with elbows around eye
  level, toes pointed, and knees slightly higher than the hips.
- In the advanced position, press the elbows down, bring the hands closer
  together, and raise the chest without losing symmetry.
- Use a fixed heading and visual reference; avoid facing directly toward or
  away from the doorway.

**Coaching and safety:** Begin at an agreed wind speed and keep the student's
altitude within their ability. Early instruction should emphasize relaxation,
symmetry, and small corrections rather than too many inputs at once.

**Links:**

- [Belly Flying Neutral Position](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YG6JhRacjfU)

## Belly Forward & Backward

**Prerequisite:** Neutral and stable belly-flying position, on or off the net.

**Desired outcome:** Move forward and backward with correct upper- and
lower-body inputs, stop between movements, and maintain altitude, stability,
heading, and control.

**Key points:**

- Start and stop basic movement by extending and retracting the arms or legs.
- Return to neutral and stop completely before beginning the next movement.
- Maintain a symmetrical arch and manage heading with small upper-body
  corrections.
- For advanced movement, combine upper- and lower-body inputs. Widen the knees
  to accelerate forward and narrow them to accelerate backward.
- Faster movement requires an earlier and stronger stopping input.

**Coaching and safety:** Establish clear stopping references and keep the
student away from a doorway-facing heading. Select an altitude and speed that
match the student's demonstrated control.

**Links:**

- [Belly Flying Forward & Backward](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HVMWTRWnDC0)

## Belly Fly Turns

**Prerequisite:** Belly-fly forward and backward movement, on or off the net.

**Desired outcome:** Complete controlled turns in both directions using
balanced upper- and lower-body inputs, beginning and ending on a predetermined
heading.

**Key points:**

- Begin and finish every turn in the neutral position.
- For a basic turn, look slightly toward the turn and lower the shoulder on
  that side while maintaining the arch.
- Apply an opposite input to stop on the selected heading.
- For advanced turns, coordinate the shoulders and knees to rotate around the
  body's center instead of orbiting around the tunnel.

**Coaching and safety:** Give the student a clear heading reference and enough
room to stop. Watch for drift, excessive banking, and loss of altitude during
the turn.

**Links:**

- [Belly Flying Left/Right Turns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LN_kPKMtHgw)

## Belly Up & Down

**Prerequisite:** Belly-fly turns plus forward and backward movement.

**Desired outcome:** Move up, stop, and move down in a controlled manner
without moving forward, backward, or changing heading.

**Key points:**

- To move up, flatten the torso and then extend the arms and legs evenly to
  increase surface area.
- Return to the neutral belly position to stop upward movement.
- To move down, increase the arch and reduce surface area by bending the arms
  and legs.
- Balance arm and leg changes to prevent unwanted drive and use small inputs to
  maintain heading.

**Coaching and safety:** This skill travels vertically through the center of
the chamber. Set an upper stopping point, manage height carefully, and begin
with small fall-rate changes before increasing the range.

**Links:**

- [Belly Flying Up & Down](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CaRpPHKutII)

## Belly Fly Side Slide

**Prerequisite:** Belly-fly up and down movement.

**Desired outcome:** Move sideways in both directions using balanced whole-body
inputs, then stop on heading without gaining or losing altitude.

**Key points:**

- Begin in neutral at the center or one side of the tunnel.
- Lower the leading-side elbow, shoulder, and knee slightly while extending the
  trailing arm.
- Balance each input to preserve heading and apply the opposite input to stop.
- For faster slides, increase whole-body pitch and begin the stopping input
  earlier.

**Coaching and safety:** Define the travel path and stopping point before the
flight. Start with small movements and increase distance or speed only after
the student demonstrates consistent heading and altitude control.

**Links:**

- [Belly Fly Side Sliding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m0vXVlzREOU)

## Belly Fly Enter & Exit

**Prerequisite:** Belly-fly turns, forward and backward movement, and up and
down movement.

**Desired outcome:** Enter into a neutral belly position and approach and exit
the doorway safely, in control, at an appropriate altitude, and with minimal
instructor assistance.

**Key points:**

- Enter from a stable stance with the chin up and hips low toward the center of
  the tunnel.
- Use arms forward and bent legs to control forward drive, then use small
  upper-body inputs to maintain a straight heading.
- For the exit, face the doorway from roughly five feet above the net and move
  forward slowly.
- Stop at the doorway before taking the frame; bend the knees to stand and step
  out instead of reaching early.

**Coaching and safety:** The tunnel instructor controls permission to enter.
Brief the doorway path and stopping point, avoid uncontrolled vertical travel,
and increase independence only after the student shows consistent control.

**Links:**

- [Belly Flying Entrance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7I0w14QxC8M)
- [Belly Flying Exit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OP3Tx0W6GaA)

## Flyer Safety Brief

**Purpose:** Establish the shared plan, equipment requirements, communication,
and safety expectations before any flight begins.

**Coordinate with the tunnel instructor:**

- Number of flyers and the skill planned for each student.
- Required instructor spotting qualification and any special assistance.
- Known wind speeds and the method for requesting adjustments.
- Length and order of each flight rotation.

**Minimum student briefing:**

- Empty pockets and remove jewelry or other loose articles.
- Confirm approved, fully fastened flight equipment: one-piece suit, suitable
  closed footwear, helmet, and goggles; gloves are optional.
- Review entrance and exit procedures.
- Explain the selected wind speed and what stability feels like on the net and
  higher in the chamber.
- Remain in the briefed flying orientation; do not copy a standing coach unless
  specifically instructed.
- If wall contact occurs, hold a strong body position and do not let the wall
  collapse or reshape it.
- Explain the tunnel instructor's role, spotting methods, and in-flight hand
  signals.

**Readiness rule:** Do not start the session until the student understands the
safety points and the coach and tunnel instructor agree on the plan.
